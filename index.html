FalseSafe-Bench

When a robot learns its own safety check, how often does it say “safe” when a crash is actually unavoidable?

In one minute: open the gym, watch the 20-second demo, and read the sentence in the top-left box. TRAP means the guard is wrong.

Robots increasingly learn their own safety checks from data, and in situations they never saw, those checks can confidently say “safe” when a crash is already unavoidable. FalseSafe-Bench measures how often and how badly that happens, by comparing each learned check with the exact right answer that a solver works out on a fine grid. You can drive it yourself in the gym, explore the true and learned answers in 3D, and look at an early, honestly-labelled test of a camera-trained “world model”.

Three takeaways

1. Padding helps, but less than hoped. Inflating uncertainty (k=2) cut false-safe volume by 40.34% [95% CI 36.47–44.73%], below the preregistered 50% bar. P1 is partially supported.
2. The errors hide where the data was not. 87.3% of the false-safe volume sits inside the hidden-physics gap, the region the model never saw.
3. Fixes work only where the model has seen data. k=2 removes 89.1% of the error outside the gap but only 33.3% inside it, and false-unsafe (over-caution) rises from 0.0001 to 0.0018.

All numbers: double integrator, 30 preregistered seeds, 201×201 grid. Source: claims_ledger.md, sweep results/sweeps/20261027_184119.

How this connects to current research

A short reading list, not results of this repo. Learned safety filters are being built from data, images and conformal statistics; FalseSafe-Bench asks the complementary question of how often each one says “safe” when it is not, measured against an exact solver.

World Models

Ha & Schmidhuber, NeurIPS 2018

VAE + MDN-RNN world model on pixel CarRacing-v0 / ViZDoom; a tiny controller is trained inside the dream.

Here: Same stack, different question: we grade how often a safety certificate computed in that dream is false-safe, against exact HJ. The gym’s Race track with a hidden ice corner is CarRacing reduced to 3D so the grid solver still applies.

Latent safety filters

Nakamura, Peters & Bajcsy, RSS 2025

HJ reachability inside a world model's latent space, learned from images.

Here: The Learned from pixels panel measures where such a latent certificate says “safe” wrongly.

Uncertainty-aware latent filters (UNISafe)

Seo, Nakamura & Bajcsy, CoRL 2025

Adds the world model's epistemic uncertainty and a conformal threshold to flag out-of-distribution states.

Here: An uncertainty-aware certifier of the kind this benchmark can grade against exact HJ ground truth on a known gap.

Conformal bounds for learned HJ value functions

Tabbara, Yang & Sibai, 2025

Uses conformal prediction to bound the gap between a learned value function and the true safety outcome; also studies ensembles of value functions.

Here: Close to the conformal C0/C1b/C2 and ensemble certifiers already in the framework.

Adaptive conformal filtering (ACoFi)

Huriot, Tabbara & Sibai, L4DC 2026

Lets the filter's switching threshold adapt online to observed prediction errors instead of staying fixed.

Here: The gym's shield trigger margin is the fixed-threshold version; an adaptive margin is an open item here.

Control barrier-value functions

Choi, Lee, Sreenath, Tomlin & Herbert, CDC 2021

Connects HJ value functions with control barrier functions.

Here: Suggests CBF-style filters as another family to grade on the same hidden-physics gaps.
